Day 3 – Saturday, November 15 – Copacabana & Fort Copacabana

Map

Lunch in Leme: A corner botequim, football on TV, picanha on a wooden board. Saturday lunch in Rio.

On the Calçadão: On foot along Copacabana. The wave pattern of the Calçadão originally came from Portugal; landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx redesigned the promenade in 1970. Along the way: a giant yellow shoe installation on the beach, jersey vendors, lifeguard posts.

↗ Learn more

Fort Copacabana: At the southern end of Copacabana lies the fort, completed in 1914, with heavy Krupp guns in armoured turrets. In 1922 the revolt of the "18 of the Fort", young officers against the government, started here – the prelude to the tenentismo movement. Today an army museum with perhaps the best view back across the whole bay to Sugarloaf Mountain.

Carlos Drummond de Andrade: On the way back, Brazil's great poet Carlos Drummond de Andrade sits on a bench in bronze – since 2002, looking out to sea. On the backrest his line: "No mar estava escrita uma cidade" – a city was written in the sea.
