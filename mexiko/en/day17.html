Day 17 – Sunday, February 5 – Chapultepec, Anthropology Museum & Farewell

Map

Bosque de Chapultepec: Last full day. At around 680 hectares, Chapultepec Park is one of the largest urban parks in Latin America. At the entrance stands the monument to the Niños Héroes: six young cadets who died in 1847 defending Chapultepec Castle against US troops.

↗ Learn more

Museo Nacional de Antropología: Mexico's most important museum, built in 1964 by Pedro Ramírez Vázquez. In the courtyard a single column carries the huge roof "El Paraguas", from which water rains down. More than 20 halls: from the Olmecs to the Aztecs, plus the living indigenous cultures of today.

↗ Learn more

Árbol de la vida: ceramic tree of life, typical of Metepec.

The Sun Stone: 24 tonnes of basalt, 3.6 m across, unearthed at the Zócalo in 1790 – the museum's most famous piece.

Diorama of the Tlatelolco market, which the Spanish conquistadors described as larger than any market in Europe.

Coatlicue, mother of the gods, with a skirt of serpents.

Olmec colossal head – around 3,000 years old.

As a farewell, concheros dance in feathered headdresses outside the museum – a tradition that lives on.

Farewell: The next morning, Monday, February 6, it's off to the airport early and back to Frankfurt. Seventeen days between the capital, the mountains, the Caribbean and the Gulf coast, three Maya cities – and a long list of places we have to come back for.
