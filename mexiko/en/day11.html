Day 11 – Monday, January 30 – Chichén Itzá & Arrival in Campeche

Map

Setting off: Breakfast under a palm roof on colourful tablecloths, then across the peninsula into the interior.

Chichén Itzá: Mexico's best-known Maya site, at its peak around AD 900–1200, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988 and one of the "New Seven Wonders" since 2007. The Pyramid of Kukulcán (El Castillo) is a calendar in stone: 4 × 91 steps plus the platform make 365. At the equinoxes a band of shadow slithers down the staircase like a serpent. Climbing has been banned since 2008.

The Temple of the Warriors with the "Group of a Thousand Columns" in front – the columns once carried a roof.

Sacred Cenote and ball court: The Sacred Cenote was a well of sacrifice: in the early 20th century gold, jade, ceramics and human bones were recovered from its bottom. At 168 m the Great Ball Court is the largest in Mesoamerica; the wall reliefs show the beheading of a player.

Campeche: On to the Gulf coast and Campeche, in time for sunset on the Malecón. After repeated pirate raids the city was fortified from the late 17th century with a wall and bastions; its colourful old town has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999.
