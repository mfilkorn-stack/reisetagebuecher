Day 3 – Sunday, January 22 – Centro Histórico: Revolución, Bellas Artes, Garibaldi

Map

Monumento a la Revolución: Brunch on a rooftop terrace overlooking the Monument to the Revolution. It was originally planned as the dome of a gigantic legislative palace under Porfirio Díaz; the Revolution stopped the project and in 1938 the steel skeleton was turned into a monument. Its pillars now hold the remains of revolutionary heroes such as Madero, Carranza and Pancho Villa.

Sunday in the Alameda Central: The Alameda is the oldest public park in the Americas (1592). On Sundays it belongs to the people of the city: couples dance under the trees, folk dancers make their skirts fly.

↗ Learn more

Palacio de Bellas Artes: Art Nouveau marble façade, Art Deco interior: construction began in 1904 under Adamo Boari and was only finished in 1934, with a revolution and money problems in between. Inside hang murals by Rivera, Siqueiros and Orozco. The heavy building has been slowly sinking into the soft lakebed for decades.

↗ Learn more

Living statues in gold-painted charro costume – a photo for a few pesos.

Calle Madero and downtown: The pedestrian street Madero links Bellas Artes with the Zócalo and is packed on Sundays. A detour into the courtyard of a colonial palace, now full of restaurants under old trees.

Plaza Garibaldi: The beating heart of mariachi music, UNESCO intangible heritage since 2011. Musicians in charro suits wait on the square for customers and play song after song for a fee in the surrounding cantinas. Bronze statues honour the great voices of ranchera music.
